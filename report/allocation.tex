\section{Combining the three strategies}\label{sec:portfolio}

\subsection{Setup}
Each strategy uses only part of its capital at any time. Option-flow selection is fully invested but dollar neutral, and the two earnings strategies hold positions only around announcements. We therefore combine them in one account and place all capital not assigned to a strategy in SOFR. With weights $w_i$ on strategy excess returns $x_{i,t}$ and the SOFR accrual $c_t$ (actual/360, at the previous session's fixing), the daily portfolio return is
\begin{equation}
  r_t \;=\; c_t + \sum_{i=1}^{3} w_i\, x_{i,t}, \qquad w_i \ge 0,\quad \sum_i w_i \le 1 .
\end{equation}
In 2024 SOFR alone returned 5.40\%; in 2022--2023 it averaged 3.37\% a year.

\subsection{Allocation rules}
Weights are estimated on 2022--2023 only and held fixed through 2024. We compare three rules.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Equal weight}: one third of capital each. Simple rules like this are hard to beat out of sample, because estimated means are noisy \citep{demiguel2009}.
  \item \textbf{Inverse volatility}: weights proportional to the reciprocal of each strategy's volatility, so each contributes similar risk.
  \item \textbf{Markowitz}: weights that maximize the expected Sharpe ratio \citep{markowitz1952}, with no strategy above 50\%. Expected returns are shrunk toward a common value \citep{jorion1986}, and covariances are shrunk toward a simpler structure \citep{ledoit2004}, both to limit estimation error.
\end{itemize}
Table~\ref{tab:weights} shows the resulting weights. Markowitz and inverse volatility both favor the pre-earnings premium because of its low volatility.

\subsection{Results}
The three strategies have low correlations with one another, so the combination is steadier than any single strategy. Table~\ref{tab:alloc} reports the results. Equal weight had a 2022--2023 Sharpe ratio of 1.28, against 0.64 to 0.96 for the individual strategies. In 2024 it returned 14.91\%, or 8.71\% over SOFR, with a maximum drawdown of $-1.68\%$.

All three rules beat SOFR in 2024 (Figure~\ref{fig:portfolio}). Equal weight did best, because it gave the most weight to the two strategies that were strongest in 2024. With one year of data, the differences between the rules are within noise. Re-estimating the Markowitz weights each month during 2024 changes little: it returns 12.51\%.

The portfolio runs at about 3--4\% volatility. Scaling equal weight to a 5\% volatility target needs 1.41 times leverage; in 2024 that would have returned 19.01\%, with a maximum drawdown of $-2.46\%$.

\subsection{Caveat and proposal}
These portfolio results overstate what an investor could have expected. We selected the three strategies after seeing their 2024 results, so 2024 is not out-of-sample for the choice of strategies. It is out-of-sample only for each strategy's rules and for the weights.

We propose equal weighting, the rule with the fewest estimated inputs, and a staged start:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Run the frozen portfolio on 2025 data without changes. Proceed only if it beats SOFR after costs and its drawdown stays under 2\%.
  \item If it passes, allocate a small risk budget, with the rest of the capital in SOFR. Stop any strategy whose rolling 63-day Sharpe ratio falls below $-1$, or whose drawdown exceeds twice its 2022--2024 maximum.
\end{enumerate}
This is a research proposal, not investment advice.
