\subsection{H5: pre-earnings implied-volatility run-up}\label{sec:h5}

\paragraph{Economic intuition and literature.}
Uncertainty about an announcement is priced gradually, so implied volatility rises into the event. Straddles bought days before and sold before the announcement can earn the run-up without bearing the event jump \citep{gaoxing2018}; see also \citet{dubinsky2019}.

\paragraph{Specification.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Signal:} an earnings calendar from \texttt{earnings\_actuals}, under the labelled assumption that announcement dates are known at least 7 sessions ahead. The exit session $X$ is the announcement date if the announcement is after the close, otherwise the session before it. Entry is at session $X-5$.
\item \emph{Portfolio:} long one ATM straddle (strike nearest $F$ from \eqref{eq:forward}) in the first expiry after the announcement. The premium budget is 0.5\% of equity per event, and events may overlap.
\item \emph{Execution tier:} T2 at $\tau = 15{:}55$ on entry and exit (buy at the ask, sell at the bid).
\item \emph{Risk limit:} the loss is bounded by the premium.
\item \emph{Secondary:} the same trade held through the event.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The engine is \file{wave2/cpp/h5_earnings.cpp}. The pre-registered rule filled 1 event, so the original primary is untestable. Amendments A1, A3 and A4 reach 75 fills. They choose the strike from quoted contracts, use a stock-mid reference when the parity forward is unavailable, and take quotes from 120 s windows. All were motivated by measured fill availability, and A4 is labelled exploratory.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample testing and results.}
The A4 variant lost 0.8\% of premium per event in development ($t = -0.81$) and 6.3\% in the holdout (11 events). Holding through the event was negative, consistent with the wave-0 EOD earnings study (\figref{fig:h5}{fig09_h5_events.png}). The decision is reject.
