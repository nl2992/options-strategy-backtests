\subsection{Waves 0 and 1: EOD defined-risk option spreads}\label{sec:w01}

\paragraph{Economic intuition and literature.}
Index options carry a variance risk premium \citep{carr2009,bollerslev2009}. Defined-risk short-premium structures might harvest it, and debit verticals might express trend. These two waves tested that idea on end-of-day data, before the intraday tape was used.

\paragraph{Specification.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Structures:} put and call credit spreads, iron condors, and call and put debit verticals on SPY, QQQ and SPX.
\item \emph{Signal and filters:} short-leg delta 0.15--0.30 and 30--45 DTE. Filters include IV percentile, implied-minus-realized volatility, trend and skew.
\item \emph{Exits:} at 50\% of the credit, a 2$\times$ loss stop, or at 5 DTE or less.
\item \emph{Execution tier:} T3, next EOD snapshot at buy-ask and sell-bid, with per-leg fees. Signals use the prior close.
\item \emph{Risk limits:} defined by construction (spread width).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The Python EOD engine is in \file{src/options_lab} (64 tests). Wave 0 covers 72 configurations with a fixed 60/20/20 split and a 2024-06 to 2024-12 test. Wave 1 covers 220 configurations with a rolling 252/63-session walk-forward (6 folds, 375 sessions). Details are in \file{reports/06_final_research_report.md} and \file{reports/rolling_research/}.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample testing and results.}
Table~\ref{tab:w01} gives the out-of-sample results. All four wave-0 structures were rejected on the fixed test window, and the deflated Sharpe ratio is about 0 over 72 trials. In wave 1, rolling selection over 220 configurations gave negative excess returns for every structure across the 6 folds. Call credit spreads were the worst, with Newey--West $t = -2.21$. Entering and exiting across the full spread cost more than any premium these structures captured.
