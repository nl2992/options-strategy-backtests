\subsection{S6: realized-minus-implied volatility straddles, exited by exercise}\label{sec:s6}

\paragraph{Economic intuition and literature.}
Options on stocks whose realized volatility exceeds implied volatility are cheap. Long-short straddle portfolios sorted on that gap earn positive returns in \citet{goyal2009}. Holding to expiry and exercising replaces the option exit spread with a stock auction trade.

\paragraph{Specification.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Signal:} on each monthly expiry Friday, for each name,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{gap} = \mathrm{RV21} - \mathrm{IV}^{\mathrm{ATM}},
\end{equation}
where RV21 is computed from closes and $\mathrm{IV}^{\mathrm{ATM}}$ is the mean IV of the call and put nearest spot in the next monthly expiry.
\item \emph{Rule:} buy ATM straddles on the 3 names with the largest gap and sell straddles on the 3 with the smallest. Enter at the EOD snapshot (buy ask, sell bid) and hold to expiry. At expiry, in-the-money legs are exercised or assigned at the official close, and the delivered shares are closed at the next opening cross (or the close if that is missing), with stock fees.
\item \emph{Portfolio:} premium-neutral, 2\% of equity of premium per side, split equally across names. A secondary buys only the top 3.
\item \emph{Execution tier:} T3 entry, with exercise at the official close and a stock auction exit (T1).
\item \emph{Risk:} early assignment of short legs is not modelled, which is a known limitation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The engine is \file{wave3/cpp/s6_straddle_gs.cpp}. With three names per side, the sort cannot reproduce the decile spreads of the original study.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample testing and results.}
The long leg lost 13.7\% of premium per trade in development, and the short leg was about 0. The long-short annual excess return is $-2.6\%$ in development (Sharpe $-0.73$, NW $t = -1.06$) and $-6.9\%$ in 2024 (Sharpe $-0.95$) (Table~\ref{tab:s6}; \figref{fig:s6s8}{fig17_s6_s8_calendar.png}). The decision is reject.
